## Place Description

In the republican period the Palatine had small but numerous gardens associated with well-appointed
houses that were on this hill by the 1st century BC. Among these numerous residences were those of
Lucius Crassus, famous for its six majestic nettle-trees (celtis australis) (Plin. N.H., 17,3-6) and Cicero’s
domus, which offered the author “all the pleasures that gardens can give” (Ad Quinct. III,1,4). This
orator’s house even included a Greek-style palaestra (laconicum) (Cic. ad Att. II,4,7), as well as an
avenue (ambulatio) for promenades (CIL VI, 29774-29775). At the beginning of the Augustan period
gardens had become almost as much a necessity for Romans as houses, and one must suppose that no
domus of any importance lacked a peripheral garden or one planted within a peristyle.
