## Province Description

An ancient district of Africa in Roman times, Mauretania lay west of Numidia and covered most of present-day Morocco and western Algeria. By the second century BCE, when Jugurtha of Numidia was rebelling against Rome, Jugurtha's father-in-law Bocchus had most of Mauretania under his control. In 25 BCE, Augustus appointed Juba II as ruler of Mauretania.

Claudius divided it into two provinces: [Mauretania Caesariensis](/id/5adf7984ef/), with Caesarea (modern Cherchel) as capital, and [Mauretania Tingitana](/id/f320d5908f/), with Tingis (modern Tangier) as capital. By the end of the 5th century CE, the province had disappeared.
