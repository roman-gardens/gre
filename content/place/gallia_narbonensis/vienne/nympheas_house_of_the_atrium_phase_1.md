## Garden Description
The elongated plan began from the east with rooms around an atrium [2] (7.5 x 6.5 m) with a square central pool. Straight ahead to the west was a peristyle [46] and on beyond a second built up area. The courtyard (15 x 9.5 m) was certainly a garden. It was surrounded on four sides by porticoes; the floor of the one on the west side was up a step from the others. The brick columns of the northern portico were covered with fluted white stucco. The peristyle had a well in the center of the east side and a rectangular pool [47] (8 x 1.5 m) with a central apse one meter in diameter at the west end in front of the reception room [33]. It could be drained by a gutter running to the east. Inside the garden, a U-shaped structure with rhythmically placed pilasters could have supported a pergola. 
Further to the west, on the axis of the pool, was a reception room (9 x 6 m) [33]. To the north and south of the peristyle spread out structures which may or may not have belonged to the house. In a trapezoidal area north of the western end of the peristyle, a second garden [29], perhaps ornamented with a pool, may have already been planted.
The house entered a second phase in the last quarter of the second century and was occupied another forty to fifty years. In the peristyle, the pool was reduced in size but embellished with water jets, the arbor was removed, and the well remodeled. The trapezoidal area [29] was now definitely a second garden. A summer triclinium (5.5 x 3 m) surrounded a semicircular pool 1.5 meters in diameter.
